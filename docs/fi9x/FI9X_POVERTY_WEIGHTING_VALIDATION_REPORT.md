# FI9-X poverty-weighting validation (provincial and LGU priority)

_Generated by `python -m app.fi9x.priority_report` from `backend/data/fi9x/out/priority_validation.json`, `priority_alternatives_province.csv` and `priority_alternatives_lgu.csv`. Production is unchanged._

## 1. Current formula and indicator weights

`app/fi5/rankings.py` (served by FI9):
* **LGU vulnerability:** V = 0.5 × percentile rank of poverty incidence (PSA 2021 city/municipal small-area estimates; Lucena City uses PSA's published 2021 HUC estimate) + 0.5 × percentile rank of population density (POPCEN 2024 population ÷ land area). Ranks are within the 142 CALABARZON LGUs.
* **LGU priority** = 100 × P(HIGH, regional outlook) × V. P is the same for every LGU, so the order is set by V alone.
* **Province priority** = population-weighted mean of its LGUs' V (× the same P).
* The equal weights were a stated normative default (OECD/JRC composite-indicator guidance). They were never estimated from an outcome.

* **Direction:** agreement rises with the poverty weight and reaches a plateau from 0.70 upward, so it does not hinge on one exact weight. Density alone ranks the provinces opposite to the survey (ρ = -0.70).
* **Time mismatch:** the formula uses 2021 poverty; the survey is 2015. Poverty 2021 vs HFIAS 2015: ρ = +0.80; poverty 2015 vs HFIAS 2015: ρ = 1.00 (FI9-X label report).
* **Strength of the reference:** none of the 10 province pairs differs significantly in the 2015 survey (largest z = 1.65, Laguna–Quezon). The survey separates Quezon from the rest only weakly, and the other four are statistically tied. Even the best scenario reaches an exact p = 0.067 with n = 5.
* **The evidence is one survey wave (2015), five provinces, HFIAS instrument.** It indicates a direction. It cannot calibrate weights.

## 5. LGU data suitability

| Indicator | Source and year | Reliability | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|

| Density | POPCEN 2024 population ÷ land area | census count; no sampling error | Measures concentration, not food-insecurity risk by itself |
| LGU food-insecurity outcome | none | – | No municipal HFIAS/FIES exists; provincial labels are **not** copied to LGUs |

* Across LGUs, poverty and density are negatively correlated (ρ = -0.53). Dense urban LGUs are less poor, so equal weights partly cancel, and density lifts urbanised, less poor LGUs.
* The number of poor people (poverty × population) correlates +0.41 with density and only +0.21 with the poverty rate. Density partly stands in for scale (how many people are affected), a dimension a rate-based index lacks.

**Reading.** More poverty weight makes top-third membership somewhat more stable but rank positions less precise, because poverty carries sampling error and density does not. Dropping the four LGUs with CV > 30 % barely changes anything. Missing data are not an issue (one substituted value, Lucena).

## 7. Limitations and unresolved evidence gaps

* **One provincial survey wave (2015), five provinces, HFIAS:** too little to estimate weights. A direction only.
* **Time gap:** 2015 survey vs 2021 poverty and 2024 density.
* **No LGU-level food-insecurity outcome:** the LGU weighting cannot be validated at all. Provincial agreement does not transfer to municipalities.
* **Imprecise LGU poverty:** 25 LGUs have SAE CVs above 20 %.
* **Density's role is conceptual, not empirical:** exposure/scale. A scale indicator such as the number of poor people may represent that dimension better (not tested against an outcome).
* **Evidence that would settle it:** provincial FIES from the 2023 NNS household file (~384 households per province); any municipal food-insecurity data (e.g. CBMS); a second FNRI wave.

## 8. Recommendation

**Partially adopt, at the provincial level only, after owner approval. Retain the current LGU formula.**

1. **Provinces: use the moderate weighting (0.65 poverty / 0.35 density).**
   * It is the smallest pre-defined change that improves agreement with the only measured provincial food-insecurity data (ρ +0.30 → +0.50).
   * It has the highest probability of being better under the survey's sampling error (0.94).
   * Density keeps a real role.
   * The strong and poverty-only versions agree more (+0.80), but choosing them would fit five statistically tied observations and let poverty dominate. They are reported as sensitivity bounds.
2. **LGUs: keep the current equal weights in production.**
   * Show the moderate-weighted rank alongside it for comparison.
   * Flag the LGUs whose top-third membership is uncertain under either formula.
   * No LGU outcome exists to justify changing local weights, and 9 of 48 top-third LGUs would change.
3. The alternatives are served read-only at `GET /api/v1/fi9/priority/alternatives` for comparison. A provincial FIES estimate from the 2023 NNS would be the next validation.
