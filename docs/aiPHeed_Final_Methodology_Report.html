aiPHeed: the methodology to use, and what it achieves

The recommended system is the E151 model: a survey-anchored LightGBM forecast that uses all five trigger groups and the two-tier news pipeline, scored on HFIAS moderate-or-severe food insecurity with HIGH at 50. It meets Specific Objectives 1 to 4 as written. It has not passed the release gates, so it stays off the dashboard.

  1. Anchor. Start from each province's last released HFIAS round, shrunk toward a level predicted from its structure. The shared national level is carried flat, never extrapolated.
  2. Adjust. LightGBM learns how far surveys moved from the anchor, from government inputs and news, with monotone signs where theory gives a direction.
  3. Score and classify. Score = 100 / (1 + e−m). HIGH at 50 or above, LOW below. Nothing is scaled, shifted or capped.
  4. Uncertainty. A 90% range from out-of-fold errors.
  5. As-of rule. Every input is used only if published before the issue date; surveys count as released 6 quarters after reference.

Why accuracy stops near 72%. Shuffled inputs still reach about 71%: most of the gain over the anchor is a correction of the overall level. Inputs barely improve ranking between provinces (0.67 vs 0.69 for the anchor). With 72 rows, a real gain needs about 6 more correct rows to be told from chance. Survey noise is not the limit; perfect regional knowledge would allow about 92%.

8. What the scores mean

  • The score is a risk score on the scale of HFIAS moderate-or-severe. It may not be called a prevalence estimate: the calibration slope interval [0.51, 0.98] excludes 1.
  • HIGH and LOW are system classes, not FNRI or FAO categories.
  • LOW means below the 50 line, not low food insecurity. In the latest surveys, 26% to 44% of households in the five provinces were cutting food, and 8% to 19% were severely food insecure.
  • Forecast levels assume the 2013 to 2021 decline continued, so Batangas and Quezon may read about 12 to 13 points low.

9. LGU rankings (Objective 3)

Priority = province probability of HIGH × vulnerability, where vulnerability = 0.6 × poverty percentile + 0.4 × density percentile. 142 LGUs, 135 distinct scores. The 0.6 / 0.4 weights are not validated: a 0.5 / 0.5 or 0.7 / 0.3 split keeps the ranking almost unchanged (Spearman 0.99), poverty alone gives 0.79 and density alone 0.67. Rank uncertainty is wide (mean 90% range of 69 places), and no municipal food-insecurity measurement exists to check it.

10. Limits a panel will ask about

  • Retrospective evidence from three survey rounds; every round has been read by earlier work.
  • False alarms: about 4 in 10 HIGH flags are wrong.
  • Employment and remittances are national and add almost nothing.
  • News is used by the model but adds no measurable accuracy beyond government data.
  • FNRI's 2023 survey used FIES, so no newer provincial HFIAS round exists to test against.

13. Wording for the thesis

A survey-anchored LightGBM model using all five trigger groups and a two-tier news pipeline reached 72.2% accuracy one quarter ahead (AUC 0.855, recall 0.76, precision 0.58) on HFIAS moderate-or-severe food insecurity at the 50 line, retrospectively across the 2018, 2019 and 2021 rounds, and 75.0% four quarters ahead. It outperformed naive persistence, PSA-only, NLP-only and logistic baselines, and halved the error of a six-year-old survey. News indicators did not add measurable accuracy beyond official data, but preceded quarterly labor and farm data during the 2020 COVID job losses and Typhoon Odette. The score is a risk score, not a prevalence estimate. Prospective validation of sealed forecasts is under way.
