# v6 Stage 3: Method credibility review

Every source below was verified this session: bibliographic details and URLs are in `results/citation_log.md`. A method whose source could not be verified, or whose assumptions fail here, is marked DROPPED.

| Method | What it is | Why it applies | Source (verified) | Failure modes at n ≈ 20–100 | Guard in this project |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rolling-origin / expanding-window evaluation | Each forecast uses only observations before its origin; the origin rolls forward | Time order matters; the series is non-stationary (2024 level shift) | Hyndman & Athanasopoulos, FPP3 §5.10; Cerqueira, Torgo & Mozetič (2020): order-preserving out-of-sample methods are most accurate for non-stationary series | Few origins, so high variance; one regime can dominate | Every P-DASH origin is used (22). Results are reported by period. **Blocked k-fold CV (Bergmeir & Benítez 2012) is NOT used**: it fits stationary series, and this one has a structural break (Cerqueira et al.). |
| Nested validation for selection | Selection and tuning only in inner folds; outer folds scored once | Prevents optimistic bias from selecting on the evaluation rows | Varma & Simon (2006) | Inner folds tiny early on, so selection unstable | Minimum 6 inner quarters before any replacement; the simplest model wins ties; the selection log is published |
| Baselines (naive / persistence, damped trend, mean) and benchmark-first evaluation | Simple references every model must beat | Avoids weak methods looking good | Hewamalage, Ackermann & Bergmeir (2023); FPP3 | Small n makes "beats baseline" noisy | Paired tests per baseline, with multiplicity control (below) |
| Gradient boosting (LightGBM) | Histogram GBDT (GOSS, EFB) | Pre-registered as a candidate because the thesis names it; not assumed | Ke et al. (2017); LightGBM docs, "Deal with Over-fitting" (small `num_leaves`, large `min_data_in_leaf`, `max_depth`, bagging, `feature_fraction`, `lambda_l1`/`lambda_l2`, `min_gain_to_split`, `extra_trees`, `path_smooth`); Parameters (`deterministic`, `force_row_wise`, `linear_tree`, `monotone_constraints`) | **Severe overfitting**: the default `min_data_in_leaf` (20) and `num_leaves` (31) assume far more rows than the 50–80 training rows here | Delta target; documented over-fitting controls; early stopping only on inner chronological folds; `deterministic` + `force_row_wise` + fixed seeds; shrinkage toward persistence; train / inner / outer gap reported (PREREGISTRATION) |
| Split-conformal / block-residual intervals | Interval half-width from past out-of-sample residuals | Distribution-free coverage | Xu & Xie (2021, EnbPI: approximate coverage without exchangeability); Gibbs & Candès (2021, adaptive under shift) | Under distribution shift, residuals from a calm period under-cover a surge | Coverage is measured and reported (N7); the residual window expands. Adaptive conformal is **not** used: it needs a long online sequence (INFERRED); documented. |
| SHAP / TreeSHAP | Additive Shapley attributions; exact for trees | Explanations must sum to the output | Lundberg & Lee (2017); Lundberg et al. (2020) | Attribution ≠ causation; correlated features share credit arbitrarily | Used only if LightGBM is served. Group sums are reported as **model attributions, not causes**. For linear or persistence models, the exact decomposition is used instead. |
| Block bootstrap for dependent data | Resample blocks of consecutive observations | Lead-2 errors overlap across issues (h = 3), so they are autocorrelated | Künsch (1989) | With 10 quarters, CIs are wide and coverage approximate | Moving blocks of 3 target quarters plus a cluster bootstrap. n is stated with every CI. |
| Paired forecast-accuracy test (DM with HLN correction) | Test of equal expected loss with a small-sample t correction | Comparison against each baseline | Diebold & Mariano (1995); Harvey, Leybourne & Newbold (1997) | Size distortion with n ≈ 10 clusters | Reported as **secondary** (squared error, HLN t with T − 1 df). The primary test is the paired block bootstrap of the within-tolerance difference. |
| Multiplicity control | Sequentially rejective Bonferroni | Several baselines are tested at once | Holm (1979) | Conservative with 8 baselines | Pre-declared α = 0.05 |
| Survey measurement error / state-space signal extraction | Observation = latent level + sampling error; Kalman filtering and smoothing | Rounds carry published sampling error | Durbin & Koopman (2012) ch. 2 (local level); Pfeffermann (2022) | Design effects unknown, so the noise variance is misspecified | κ multiplier (LL-K) estimated in-fold. The design effect is **requested from SWS** and never estimated from scored outcomes for the tolerance. |
