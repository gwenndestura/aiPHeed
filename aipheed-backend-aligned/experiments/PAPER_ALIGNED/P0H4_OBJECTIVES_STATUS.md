## Where P0H4 differs from the paper (applies to every row)

| Paper | P0H4 | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Target: Equation 3 composite score (SWS hunger + 2 × provincial food-CPI deviation), HIGH above the **global median** of 120 province-quarters (§3.4.2) | HIGH when Balance Luzon SWS hunger > 11.3% **and** the province's food CPI (or food minus headline) is above its **fixed 2021–22 60th percentile** | Same two sources as §1.7. The thresholds are fixed in advance, so no label uses test-period data (Rule 3). It is not Equation 3. |
| One Optuna-tuned LightGBM (Algorithm 4: 100 TPE trials, holdout F1 ≥ 0.75 and AUC ≥ 0.80) | P(HIGH) = P(hunger) × P(price). The price part is one LightGBM across all leads, with configuration chosen by inner chronological validation over a fixed 8-setting grid. The hunger part is closed-form, from the latest SWS reading | No Optuna and no Algorithm 4 holdout. It is a hybrid, not a single LightGBM. |
| Reliability: Algorithm 3, mean fold accuracy ≥ 0.75 | Reported under Algorithm 3 **and** under the stricter frozen R1–R7 | Maximum Reliable Lead Time **2** (Algorithm 3) / **1** (R1–R7) |
| Province-level food insecurity | Provinces differ through the food-price part only. SWS hunger is regional (Balance Luzon) | "Province-level" holds for the risk class, not for a measured provincial hunger rate. |

## B. Hypotheses (§1.4, pp. 11–12)

Each hypothesis is a null hypothesis. "Rejected" supports the system.

| # | Exact wording | Status | Evidence | Plainly |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | "The Food Stress Sentiment Index (FSSI) and food CPI deviation will not be among the strongest predictors of food insecurity risk in CALABARZON when examined through SHAP feature attribution." | **Partially achieved: rejected for food CPI deviation, not rejected for FSSI** | A1 (projected food-CPI deviation from the province's usual level) is ranked **1st** (46.8%). N1 (FSSI-v3) is ranked 5th (6.2%). It is present in only 17% of province-quarters (20 of 115 SUFFICIENT). With vs without news, the Lead-1 Brier changes by −0.012 (Holm p 0.86) (`../DASHBOARD_SELECTION/DASHBOARD_SELECTION_REPORT.md` §5) | Food-price deviation is the strongest predictor. FSSI is not, and news does not measurably improve the forecast. |
| H2 | "aiPHeed will not reliably classify whether a CALABARZON province is at high or low food insecurity risk across multiple forward-looking lead times." | **Partially achieved: rejected under the paper's 75% rule (Leads 1–2); not rejected under R1–R7 (Lead 1 only)** | Leads 1 and 2 pass the 75% rule (0.843, 0.769); Lead 3 fails (0.717). R1–R7 pass at Lead 1 only. Lead 2 fails R3 (balanced 0.635), R5 (vs climatology p 0.146) and R6 (recall 0.353) | Reliable across two lead times by the paper's rule, one by the stricter rule. | H3 | "The LightGBM model that combines news signals and government data will not outperform the six comparison models, naive persistence, three logistic regression variants (news-only, government data-only, and combined), and two LightGBM variants trained on only one data source at a time, in classifying provincial food insecurity risk across CALABARZON." | **Not rejected** | LGBM-GOV is more accurate (0.914 vs 0.843) and has a significantly lower Brier score at Lead 1 (P0H4 − LGBM-GOV +0.045, p 0.027) | P0H4 does not outperform all six. The government-only LightGBM is better. |
| H4 | "The vulnerability-weighted spatial disaggregation of province-level forecasts will not produce municipal and city risk rankings that are consistent with DA Region IV-A evaluators' assessments of relative food insecurity vulnerability across CALABARZON, as evaluated through the aiPHeed Post-Survey Questionnaire." | **Waiting for evaluators** | Post-survey not held | The test needs the DA questionnaire. |
