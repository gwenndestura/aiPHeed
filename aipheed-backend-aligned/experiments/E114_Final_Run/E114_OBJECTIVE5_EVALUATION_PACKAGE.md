# E114 Objective 5: DA IV-A user-evaluation package (ready to administer)

**Purpose.** Chapter I Objective 5 asks for the dashboard to be evaluated by DA IV-A staff, using ISO/IEC 25010 quality characteristics. This package can be run without any further design work. It cannot be run from here, because it needs the respondents.

**Respondents.** 50 DA Region IV-A staff (the thesis sample).

| Group | Planned n | Who |
|---|---|---|
| A | 10 | Planning and monitoring officers (primary users of the outlook) |
| B | 25 | Program staff (rice, livestock, fisheries, HVCDP) |
| C | 10 | Field and LGU-facing staff (Agricultural Program Coordinating Officers) |
| D | 5 | ICT and records staff (functional suitability, compatibility) |

**Time per respondent:** about 35 minutes (10 scenarios, 15 questionnaire, 10 debrief).

---

## 1. Session plan

| Step | Minutes | What the facilitator does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | Consent; explain this tests the tool, not the person; no right answers |
| 2 | 2 | Show the one-page plain-language summary (E114) |
| 3 | 10 | Run the scenarios below; respondent thinks aloud; facilitator records success, time and errors on the observation sheet |
| 4 | 15 | Respondent completes sections A–E |
| 5 | 5 | Debrief questions F1–F3 |

---

## 2. Scenario walkthrough (task-based)

**Scoring.** Each scenario is scored success, partial or fail, with time to completion. Facilitators use the answer key and do not help.

| # | Task given to the respondent | Correct answer (answer key) | What it tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | "What is the latest measured hunger level for Balance Luzon, and from which survey quarter?" | The value and its source quarter shown on the outlook card | Clarity of the outlook |
| S2 | "How far ahead is this outlook from the survey it is based on?" | The true horizon (for example "2 quarters after the newest survey") | Understanding of the true horizon |
| S3 | "Give the range the next reading will most likely fall in." | The 80% band shown | Interpretation of uncertainty |
| S4 | "Is the dashboard predicting a rise in hunger next quarter?" | "No. The outlook carries the latest reading forward; it does not predict direction." | Not over-reading the forecast |
| S5 | "Which municipality in Quezon has the highest measured hunger?" | "The tool has no municipal hunger measurement. The table ranks vulnerability (poverty and density)." | Correct reading of the LGU table |
| S6 | "Did disaster news rise this quarter compared with a year ago?" | Read from the news context panel | Use of news context |
| S7 | "A typhoon hit last month. Should you expect the outlook to change?" | "Only when a new SWS reading is published; check the news panel for context." | Decision relevance and limits |
| S8 | "Where would you check whether food prices are rising?" | The CPI context panel, labelled as separate from hunger | Separating prices from hunger |

## 3. Questionnaire

### Section A: System Usability Scale (SUS; Brooke 1996)

Scale: 1 = strongly disagree … 5 = strongly agree. Use the standard wording; a Filipino translation may be added and back-translated.

1. I think that I would like to use this system frequently.
2. I found the system unnecessarily complex.
3. I thought the system was easy to use.
4. I think that I would need the support of a technical person to be able to use this system.
5. I found the various functions in this system were well integrated.
6. I thought there was too much inconsistency in this system.
7. I would imagine that most people would learn to use this system very quickly.
8. I found the system very cumbersome to use.
9. I felt very confident using the system.
10. I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with this system.

**Scoring.**
- Odd items: score − 1. Even items: 5 − score.
- Sum the ten item scores and multiply by 2.5, giving 0–100.
- Report the mean, SD and 95% CI. Compare with the benchmark of 68 (average usability).

### Section B: Usefulness and decision relevance

Scale: 1–5 Likert.

B1. The hunger outlook is useful for my unit's quarterly planning.
B2. The outlook arrives early enough to inform decisions.
B3. The news context panel helps me understand what is happening in the region.
B4. The food-price (CPI) panel helps me anticipate pressure on household food access.
B5. The LGU vulnerability ranking helps me prioritise municipalities for programs.
B6. I would use this tool when preparing briefings for management.

### Section C: Clarity and understanding

C1. I understand what the hunger number measures (SWS families who experienced hunger in the last 3 months).
C2. I understand what "true horizon" means.
C3. I understand what the range (80% band) means.
C4. The dashboard makes clear what it can and cannot predict.
C5. The difference between the hunger outlook and the vulnerability ranking is clear.

### Section D: Trust and transparency

D1. I trust the hunger figures shown (they come from SWS).
D2. I trust the range shown around the outlook.
D3. The dashboard is honest about its limitations.
D4. I would be comfortable citing the outlook in an official memo, with its stated limits.

### Section E: ISO/IEC 25010 quality characteristics

Scale: 1–5.

| Characteristic | Item |
|---|---|
| Functional suitability | E1. The tool provides the functions I need for food-security monitoring. |
| Performance efficiency | E2. Pages load quickly enough for my work. |
| Compatibility | E3. The outputs can be used with our existing reports and tools (for example exported tables). |
| Interaction capability (usability) | E4. I can find what I need without help. |
| Reliability | E5. The tool worked without errors during this session. |
| Security | E6. I am confident the tool handles access appropriately. |
| Maintainability | E7. (Group D only) Updating the data each quarter appears manageable. |
| Flexibility/portability | E8. The tool works on the devices I use at work. |

### Section F: Open debrief

F1. What would you use this tool for in the next quarter?
F2. What, if anything, could be misread or misused?
F3. What one change would make it more useful to DA IV-A?

## 4. Analysis plan (pre-specified)

**Scores to report.**
- **SUS:** mean, SD and 95% CI overall and by group A–D. "Acceptable" means a mean ≥ 68.
- **Sections B–E:** item mean, median and the % agreeing (4–5), overall and by group.
- **Scenarios:** success rate per scenario, with a target of ≥ 80% success. S4 and S5 are critical-misreading checks; any failure rate above 20% triggers a wording change on the dashboard before deployment.

**Consistency and open answers.**
- **Internal consistency:** Cronbach's α for B, C, D and E (report; α ≥ 0.70 acceptable).
- **Open answers:** thematic coding by two researchers; report agreement (Cohen's κ).

**What is not done.** No inferential claim that the tool improves decisions. The evaluation measures usability, understanding and perceived usefulness.

---

## 5. Who evaluates what, and when

| When | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Researchers | Pilot with 3 non-sample DA staff; fix wording; freeze the instrument |
| Weeks 2–3 | Researchers + DA IV-A focal person | Sessions with groups A–D (in person or video call, screen shared) |
| Week 4 | Researchers | Score SUS and ISO items; code open answers; write the Objective 5 section |
| After the next SWS release | DA IV-A planning unit | Optional follow-up: use the re-issued outlook in a real briefing and record whether it was used |

**Materials:**
- the dashboard (read-only build);
- the E114 one-page summary;
- the observation sheet (scenario × respondent grid);
- this questionnaire (printed or Google Form).

**Ethics:**
- informed consent;
- no personal data beyond the respondent group;
- responses anonymised.
