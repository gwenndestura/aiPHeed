## Chapter III (Methodology)

**S-III-1. Two targets, named separately.** *(Where the forecasting target or label is defined.)*

> The implemented system classifies each province using the CPI-based label defined in Equation 4, which marks a quarter as elevated when the provincial food price index stays above its recent trend. The research evaluation additionally examined a corrected form of the composite stress indicator in Equation 3, referred to as S1, which combines the newly released Balance-Luzon hunger estimate with the province's relative food inflation. The two targets measure disparate concepts, and results obtained for one are not transferred to the other.

**S-III-2. The served model, described exactly.** *(Where the model is described.)*

> The implemented system serves a blended classifier that combines a LightGBM model (40 percent), a logistic regression on government indicators (40 percent), and a logistic regression on news-derived triggers (20 percent), with a decision threshold of 0.712 stored in the model bundle.

Confirm 0.712 against the bundle before submission. It is recorded in earlier project records, not re-verified here.

**S-III-3. The classification rule and missing evidence.**

> The dashboard displays HIGH or LOW for every province and municipality. The research rule additionally withholds a classification, reporting INSUFFICIENT_DATA, when a required input is missing or stale. This rule is part of the evaluated research method and not of the implemented dashboard.

**S-III-4. Municipal values.**

> Municipal values will be generated by using a disaggregation that allocates each provincial forecast according to LGU poverty incidence (60 percent) and population density (40 percent). These values are indicative and not definitive determinations, because no municipal measurement of hunger or food prices exists against which they could be validated.

**S-III-5. Evaluation design and its limits.** Add the E9 design text from `E9/DRAFT_CHAPTER_III_E9.md`, with one sentence on reuse:

## Chapter IV (Results and Discussion)

**S-IV-1. Label the results by system.** Present the production model's figures (its holdout and walk-forward results) and the E9 research results in separate subsections with separate headings. Do not merge them into one performance claim.

**S-IV-2. The accuracy target.** Replace any statement that the system meets a 75% target with:

> Because 33 of the 35 test outcomes were HIGH, a rule that always predicts HIGH reaches 94.3 percent accuracy. Accuracy therefore cannot indicate forecasting skill in this period, and the evaluation relied on balanced accuracy, class-specific recall, and the error of the predicted stress values. Under these measures, no approach demonstrated a clear advantage over the simple formula-based forecast.

## Chapter V (Conclusions and Recommendations)

**S-V-1. Conclusion.**

> The study demonstrates that a transparent formula combining regional hunger and relative provincial food inflation can be computed from newly released public data and used as a forecasting rule. The machine learning models examined did not establish an advantage over this rule with the seven quarters available.

**S-V-2. Recommendations:**

1. collect province-level food insecurity outcomes to validate the weight of 2 and the target itself;
2. extend the series with each newly released quarter;
3. verify RSSO IV-A provincial release dates;
4. complete the prospective 2026-Q4 evaluation under the frozen protocol (`05_Q4_2026_PROSPECTIVE_PROTOCOL.md`).
