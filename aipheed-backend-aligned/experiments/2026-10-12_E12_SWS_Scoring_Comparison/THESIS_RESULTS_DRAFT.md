## Composition of the Stress Indicator

The study examined how the regional hunger component and the provincial food-price component contribute to the composite stress indicator. Because the Social Weather Stations (SWS) Balance-Luzon hunger estimate is a single regional value, adding it with any weight raises or lowers all five provincial scores by the same amount. As a result, the order of the provinces within a quarter, and the gap between the highest and lowest province, depend only on each province's food inflation relative to the five-province mean.

The study verified this property on a synthetic test case and on all 36 weight combinations examined. Every combination produced the same provincial ranking as the relative food-price deviation alone. The 36 combinations reduce to 22 mathematically distinct weight ratios, and five of them are rescaled versions of the original indicator.

Standardizing the two components did not change this result. It only converted the chosen weights into a ratio that varied across the training periods, from 0.35 to 0.90 for equal weights.

## Regional and Provincial Information

Regional hunger accounted for most of the indicator's behaviour:

- **65 percent** of the variation in the composite score was shared by all five provinces in a quarter.
- **76 percent** of the province-quarters received the same HIGH or LOW classification that the regional hunger value alone would have given.

The provincial food-price deviation was available for all five provinces in all 20 quarters from 2021 to 2025. It identified meaningful differences in relative price pressure in every quarter. The ranking of provinces, however, changed substantially over time. Quezon moved from the lowest relative pressure in 2023 to the highest in 2024 and early 2025.

## Forecasting the Constructed Indicator

When each weighting was used to forecast the next quarter's composite indicator, combinations that placed relatively less weight on the latest provincial deviation produced lower errors than the original weighting. The mean absolute error was 3.27 to 3.47 for these combinations, against 4.63 for the original after the same training-only rescaling.

This result is indicative and not definitive. It describes how to forecast the constructed indicator, and not how to measure food insecurity, because the forecasting target is defined by the same formula. The weighting that performed best was also found by examining the same seven quarters used in earlier exploratory analyses.

## Interpretation

No province-level measurement of food insecurity is available against which the weights or thresholds could be validated. The study therefore cannot establish which formula best represents actual provincial food insecurity. The evidence supports reporting the two components as separate layers:

- **Regional layer:** the Balance-Luzon hunger level and its trend.
- **Provincial layer:** each province's relative food-price pressure and ranking.

The composite indicator is retained as the documented measure of the thesis, with the limitation that its HIGH or LOW status mainly reflects the regional hunger level.
