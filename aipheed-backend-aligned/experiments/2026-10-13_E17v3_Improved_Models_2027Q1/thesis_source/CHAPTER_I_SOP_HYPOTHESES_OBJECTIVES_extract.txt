1.3 Statement of the Problem
         CALABARZON's food insecurity monitoring relies entirely on periodic surveys that are

years outdated by the time shocks occur. This study aims to develop aiPHeed, a geospatial food
insecurity forecasting system for DA Region IV-A using NLP and machine learning to support
anticipatory intervention and decision-making.

         Specifically, this study seeks to answer the following questions:
    1. What variables most significantly predict food insecurity risk across the provinces of

         CALABARZON, as identified through SHAP feature attribution?
    2. What is the furthest lead time at which aiPHeed can reliably forecast province-level food

         insecurity risk across CALABARZON?
    3. How does the LightGBM model combining NLP-derived news signals and government

         statistical indicators compare against six baseline approaches , naive persistence, three
         logistic regression variants (combined features, government indicators only, and NLP
         features only), and two LightGBM ablation variants trained on each data source
         separately , in classifying provincial food insecurity risk?
    4. How effectively do the city- and municipality-level food insecurity risk rankings derived
         from province-level forecasts reflect relative vulnerability across CALABARZON?

         personnel, as usability evaluators, assess aiPHeed under the ISO/IEC 25010 software
         quality model, in terms of functional accuracy and ease of use respectively?

1.4 Hypothesis
         This study tests five null hypotheses, each addressing the relationship between an

independent variable and a dependent variable. The independent variables are the news-derived
signals and the government socioeconomic indicators. The dependent variable is the binary food
insecurity risk classification, either high or low, of a CALABARZON province in a given
quarter. The study's assumptions are based on the null hypothesis that these independent
variables do not carry predictive signals strong enough for aiPHeed to classify, forecast,
disaggregate, or be judged useful for that purpose.

         Specifically, the study sought to address the following:

    1. The Food Stress Sentiment Index (FSSI) and food CPI deviation will not be among the
         strongest predictors of food insecurity risk in CALABARZON when examined through
         SHAP feature attribution.

    2. aiPHeed will not reliably classify whether a CALABARZON province is at high or low
         food insecurity risk across multiple forward-looking lead times.

    3. The LightGBM model that combines news signals and government data will not
         outperform the six comparison models, naive persistence, three logistic regression
         variants (news-only, government data-only, and combined), and two LightGBM variants
         trained on only one data source at a time, in classifying provincial food insecurity risk
         across CALABARZON.

1.5 Research Objectives

1.5.1 General Objective

  To develop, validate, and deploy aiPHeed: A Geospatial Food Insecurity

Forecasting System for DA Region IV-A Using NLP and Machine Learning.

1.5.2 Specific Objectives
    1. To design a two-tier pipeline combining zero-shot XLM-RoBERTa
         Filipino-English sentiment extraction and BERTopic unsupervised topic modeling
         to produce the bias-corrected Food Stress Sentiment Index and latent food
         insecurity risk patterns per province-quarter from a Filipino-English news corpus.
    2. To evaluate the performance of the proposed LightGBM forecasting model using
         the following metrics:
           I. Accuracy, to measure overall correctness of high- and low-risk
                  province-level classifications
          II. Precision and Recall, to assess the model's ability to correctly identify
                  high-risk provinces while minimizing false alarms and missed detections
                  respectively

     III. F1-Score, to capture the balance between Precision and Recall given the
             binary classification task

     IV. AUC-ROC, to evaluate the model's ability to distinguish between high-
             and low-risk provinces across classification thresholds

      V. Forecast performance across multiple lead times via walk-forward
             expanding-window cross-validation to empirically determine the furthest
             horizon at which reliable forecasting is achievable

3. To produce spatially disaggregated municipal and city food insecurity risk
    rankings from validated province-level forecasts, using PSA published poverty
    incidence and population density as vulnerability weights.

4. To compare the effectiveness of the proposed LightGBM forecasting model
    against the following baseline approaches:
       I. Naive persistence baseline
      II. PSA-only indicator baseline
     III. NLP-only sentiment baseline
     IV. Logistic regression baseline
