  ENSO ADVISORY
El Niño Advisory No. 5

A strong to very strong El Niño is present in the Tropical Pacific. Forecasts predict that El
Niño will continue until the first half of 2027 and may reach its peak before the end of
2026.

El Niño increases the likelihood of below-normal rainfall, which may lead to adverse
impacts, including dry spells and droughts in some areas of the country. However, above-
normal rainfall conditions may still occur during the passage of tropical cyclones and other
rain-bearing weather systems.

Assessment for September 2026

The weather systems that influenced the weather and climate of the country during the
month were the Southwest (SW) Monsoon, easterlies, localized thunderstorms, low-
pressure areas (LPAs), intertropical convergence zone (ITCZ), and the passage of two
(2) tropical cyclones (TCs) in the Philippine Area of Responsibility (PAR): Tropical Storm
(TS) "Pilandok" (KROVANH) from August 30 to September 03, and Typhoon (TY)
"Queenie" (SURIGAE) from September 24-27.

The rainfall assessment for the month showed that most areas of the country experienced
below- to near-normal rainfall conditions. However, patches of above-normal rainfall were
observed in Benguet, La Union, Pangasinan, Bataan, Zambales, and Cavite. Way below-
normal rainfall conditions were also observed in the Negros Provinces.

In addition, seven (7) provinces experienced meteorological drought; three (3) provinces
were under dry spell; and thirty (30) provinces experienced dry conditions. For a complete
list of these provinces, please refer to the meteorological Drought/Dry Spell
Assessment Maps and Tables or the PH Meteorological Drought Monitor, both
available on the official DOST-PAGASA website.

Slightly above- to above-average mean surface air temperatures were observed over
most of the country during the month, except for select areas in Luzon, including Baguio,
Camarines Sur, and Northern Palawan, where cooler-than-average temperatures were
recorded. The observed temperature ranges were as follows: mountainous areas of
Luzon (13.4°C ­ 25.2°C); Metro Manila (23.8°C ­ 35.1°C); rest of Luzon (19.8°C ­
36.8°C); Visayas (22.5°C ­ 37.7°C); mountainous areas of Mindanao (17.5°C ­ 33.5C);
rest of Mindanao (20.9°C ­ 37.0°C). Meanwhile, four (4) stations have surpassed their
highest maximum temperature record for September: Catbalogan, Samar on September
2 (37.2°C); Borongan, Eastern Samar on September 3 (37.7°C); Laguindingan Airport,
Misamis Oriental on September 4 (37.0°C), and Zamboanga City on September 11
(36.2°C).

Outlook for October 2026

The weather systems expected to affect the country in October are the ITCZ, LPAs,
localized thunderstorms, easterlies, ridge of the high-pressure area (HPA), and the
occurrence of one (1) to two (2) TCs that may develop within or enter the PAR.
Meanwhile, a transition to the Northeast (NE) Monsoon is likely this month.

The rainfall forecast for October suggests that below-normal rainfall conditions will be
experienced over most parts of Luzon, Visayas, and Caraga Region, while near-normal
rainfall conditions are likely over Batanes, Cagayan, Negros Island Region, Central
Visayas, and the rest of Mindanao.

Based on observed rainfall during the past months and the forecast for October,
meteorological drought/dry spells are likely to be experienced by the end of the month in
most parts of Bicol Region, Eastern Visayas, Surigao del Norte and Dinagat Islands.
Meanwhile, dry conditions are likely over Abra, Kalinga, Isabela, Quezon, and most of
MIMAROPA and Western Visayas.

Meanwhile, near-average to warmer-than-average mean air temperatures are expected
over most parts of the country, while slightly cooler-than-average temperatures over some
parts of MIMAROPA and Bohol. The ranges of forecast temperature are as follows:
13.1°C ­ 26.7°C in the mountainous areas of Luzon, 21.1°C ­ 36.0°C for Metro Manila,
17.0°C ­ 37.2°C over the rest of Luzon, 21.2°C ­ 36.5°C in Visayas, 15.9°C ­ 33.7°C in
the mountainous areas of Mindanao, and 20.6°C ­ 37.7°C over the rest of Mindanao.

DOST-PAGASA will continue to closely monitor the ongoing El Niño and its associated
impacts on the country's climate. Meanwhile, all government agencies and the general
public are strongly advised to take appropriate preparedness and response measures to
mitigate the impacts of El Niño, particularly in areas vulnerable to dry spells and warmer
temperatures.
