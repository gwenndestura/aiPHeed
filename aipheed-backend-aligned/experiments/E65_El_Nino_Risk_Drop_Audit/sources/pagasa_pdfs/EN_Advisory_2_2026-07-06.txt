                                              ENSO ADVISORY
                                           El Niño Advisory No. 2

A weak to moderate El Niño continues to be present in the tropical Pacific. Climate models
suggest that El Niño will further intensify, becoming very strong by October-November-
December 2026 season.

El Niño increases the likelihood of below-normal rainfall conditions, which may result in
adverse impacts such as dry spells and droughts in some areas of the country. However,
over the western part of the country, above-normal rainfall conditions during the
Southwest monsoon season (i.e., Habagat) may also occur.

Assessment for June 2026

The onset of the rainy season was declared on June 04, following the occasional to
frequent rainfall observed at selected DOST-PAGASA synoptic stations due to the
Southwest (SW) monsoon.

The weather systems that affected the country during the month were the SW Monsoon,
easterlies, localized thunderstorms, low-pressure areas (LPAs), intertropical
convergence zone (ITCZ), and the passage of three (3) tropical cyclones (TCs) in the
Philippine Area of Responsibility (PAR): Tropical Depression (TD) "Ester" from 05 to 06
June, Super Typhoon (STY) "Francisco" (MEKKHALA) from 20 to 25 June and Sever
Tropical Storm (STS) "Gardo" (HIGOS) from 25 to 26 June. While, TD "Ester" did not
affect any part of the country, STY "Francisco" and STS "Gardo" enhanced the Southwest
Monsoon, resulting in heavy rainfall over the western sections of the country.

The rainfall assessment for the month showed that most areas of the country experienced
below- to near-normal rainfall conditions. However, patches of above-normal rainfall were
observed in Palawan and the Davao region, while way-below normal rainfall was
experienced in Camarines Norte, Siquijor, Surigao del Sur, and Basilan.

Further, based on the said rainfall assessment, ten (10) provinces experienced
meteorological drought; twenty-one (21) provinces were under dry spells, and one (1)
province experienced dry conditions. For a complete list of these provinces, please refer
to the Drought/Dry Spell Assessment Maps and Tables.

Slightly warmer to warmer-than-average mean surface air temperatures were observed
across most parts of the country during the month, except for select areas in western
Luzon, Bicol, and southern Mindanao where cooler-than-average temperatures were
recorded. The recorded temperature ranges were as follows: mountainous areas of Luzon
(15.1°C ­ 26.8°C); rest of Luzon (16.2°C ­ 39.1°C); Visayas (22.4°C ­ 36.8°C);
mountainous areas of Mindanao (19.4°C ­ 36.5°C); rest of Mindanao (22.0°C ­ 37.0°C);
and Metro Manila (21.5°C ­ 36.7°C). Meanwhile, no extreme temperature records were
surpassed in June 2026.

Outlook for July 2026

The weather systems expected to affect the weather and climate of the country in July
are the Southwest Monsoon, easterlies, LPAs, ITCZ, localized thunderstorms, ridge of
the HPA, and the occurrence of two (2) to four (4) tropical cyclones (TCs) that may
develop within or enter the PAR.

The rainfall forecast for July shows that above-normal rainfall is expected in the northern
and western parts of Luzon, and below-normal rainfall are likely in western and eastern
Mindanao, while near-normal conditions are expected across the rest of the country. This
is supported by the probabilistic forecast, suggesting higher chances of above-normal
rainfall in the northern and western parts of Luzon, while higher chances of below-normal
rainfall in most parts of Mindanao.

Meanwhile, near-average to warmer-than-average mean temperatures are expected
across most parts of the country, with areas likely to experience slightly cooler-than-
average conditions. The ranges of forecast temperature are as follows: 13.4°C ­ 27.2°C
in the mountainous areas of Luzon, 20.0°C ­ 37.0°C over the rest of Luzon, 21.0°C ­
36.3°C in Visayas, 15.8°C ­ 32.6°C in the mountainous areas of Mindanao, 15.8°C ­
37.5°C over the rest of Mindanao, and 21.3°C ­ 37.2°C for Metro Manila.

DOST-PAGASA will continue to closely monitor the ongoing El Niño and its associated
impacts on the country's climate. Meanwhile, all government agencies and the general
public are strongly advised to take appropriate preparedness and response measures to
mitigate their potential impacts, particularly in areas vulnerable to heavy rainfall and floods
during Habagat season and those areas prone to below-normal rainfall conditions.
