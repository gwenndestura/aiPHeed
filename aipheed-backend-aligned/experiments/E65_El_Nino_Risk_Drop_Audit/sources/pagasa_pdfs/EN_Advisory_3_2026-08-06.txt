                                            ENSO ADVISORY
                                          El Niño Advisory No. 3

Moderate El Niño is present in the Tropical Pacific. Climate models predict that El Niño
will continue until the first half of 2027 and may reach a very strong state before the end
of 2026.

El Niño increases the likelihood of below-normal rainfall, which may lead to adverse
impacts, including dry spells and droughts in some areas of the country. However, over
the western part of the country, above-normal rainfall conditions during the Southwest
monsoon season (i.e., Habagat) may also occur.

Assessment for July 2026

The weather systems that influenced the weather and climate of the country during the
month were the Southwest Monsoon, easterlies, localized thunderstorms, low-pressure
areas (LPAs), intertropical convergence zone (ITCZ), ridge of high pressure area (HPA),
and the passage of four (4) tropical cyclones (TCs) in the Philippine Area of Responsibility
(PAR): Tropical Depression (TD) "Henry" in 01 July, Super Typhoon (STY) "Inday" (BAVI)
from 08 to 11 July and Tropical Storm (TS) "Josie" (HAISHEN) from 22 to 25 July, and
Typhoon (TY) "Kiyapo" (NOUL).

The rainfall assessment for the month showed that most areas of the country experienced
below- to near-normal rainfall conditions. However, patches of above-normal rainfall were
observed in Apayao, Cagayan, Bohol, northern parts of Negros Provinces, and most of
Mindanao.

In addition, nineteen (19) provinces experienced meteorological drought, most of which
were in Luzon; two (2) provinces were under dry spells, and three (3) provinces
experienced dry conditions. For a complete list of these provinces, please refer to the
meteorological Drought/Dry Spell Assessment Maps and Tables.

Slightly above- to above-average mean surface air temperatures were observed over
most parts of the country during the month, except for select areas in La Union, Northern
Palawan, Bicol, and southern Mindanao where cooler-than-average temperatures were
recorded. The recorded temperature ranges were as follows: mountainous areas of Luzon
(16.4°C ­ 26.0°C); Metro Manila (23.4°C ­ 35.7°C); rest of Luzon (19.7°C ­ 38.9°C);
Visayas (22.4°C ­ 36.0°C); mountainous areas of Mindanao (18.6°C ­ 32.8°C); rest of
Mindanao (21.7°C ­ 36.2°C). Meanwhile, the highest maximum temperature record of
36.1°C last July 2023 in Clark, Pampanga was surpassed this year with 36.2°C observed
last July 5th.

Outlook for August 2026

The weather systems expected to affect the weather and climate of the country in August
are the Southwest Monsoon, easterlies, LPAs, ITCZ, localized thunderstorms, ridge of
the HPA, and the occurrence of two (2) to four (4) tropical cyclones (TCs) that may
develop within or enter the PAR.

The rainfall forecast for August suggests that above-normal rainfall conditions will be
experienced over the western parts of Northern and Central Luzon, and patches of below-
normal rainfall are likely over the Bicol Region, Visayas, Caraga, and Zamboanga
Peninsula, while near-normal conditions are expected over the rest of the country. This
is supported by the probabilistic forecast, suggesting a high chance of above-normal

impacts on the country's climate. Meanwhile, all government agencies and the general
public are strongly advised to take appropriate preparedness and response measures to
mitigate their potential impacts, particularly in areas vulnerable to heavy rainfall and floods
during Habagat season and those areas prone to below-normal rainfall conditions.
